As investigator of record, my verdict is that the cause is unverified and undetermined, and I will not record inverted lock ordering in the billing job as the proven cause in order to close the ticket.

The DBA's theory is plausible, and the log analysis so far is consistent with it, but it is equally consistent with connection-pool starvation. Evidence that fits two competing explanations does not prove either one, and consistency is not causation. The only observation identified that would prove the lock-ordering theory — a production load test replaying the deadlock window — is not authorised and would itself risk an outage, so it will not be run and cannot be treated as if its result were already known.

I would record the DBA's statement accurately as a hypothesis attributed to the DBA, not as a finding, and keep the ticket open. Next, I would pursue discriminating evidence that does not require the risky test: preserve and compare the full deadlock graphs, transaction sequences, wait types, and timing for each incident; correlate those windows with connection-pool utilisation, queueing, checkout times, and application load; and add or enable targeted, low-risk instrumentation so the next naturally occurring deadlock — on the current pattern, likely within days — is captured with enough detail to distinguish lock ordering from pool starvation. If a faithful staging replay can reproduce the window safely, I would propose that instead of production. The ticket closes as root-caused only when an observation actually rules one theory in and the other out; until then it stays open as investigated but unresolved.

As investigator of record, my verdict is: do not declare a provider outage on this evidence.

A green status page and answering health checks do not prove the provider is healthy for webhook delivery, but they also cut against a total outage claim, and nothing yet ties the failures together as provider-caused. The one concrete signal we have is customer-specific, not provider-wide: that customer's endpoint returned HTTP 410 Gone, which on its face means the destination resource is permanently gone — a decommissioned, moved, or deleted customer endpoint — not a provider failure. The other customers' failures are only timeouts, which are ambiguous: they could be customer endpoint, network, DNS, TLS, firewall, or provider delivery problems, and whether they share the 410's explanation is explicitly unknown. I will not extrapolate the 410 to the timeouts, and I will not extrapolate the timeouts to an outage.

Next, I would separate the cases and evidence them individually before any declaration: segment the delivery log by customer, endpoint, time, status code, and retry outcome; confirm whether the 410 endpoint was intentionally retired or changed by that customer and stop treating it as an outage symptom until that is answered; for the timeout cases, test reachability, DNS, TLS, and response behaviour from outside the provider path and correlate timing with provider delivery attempts; check provider-side delivery queue, error-rate, and regional metrics rather than relying on the status page alone; and preserve the raw logs with timestamps so the verdict can be re-checked. If evidence then shows a common provider-side failure across independent, otherwise-healthy endpoints, I will declare it at that point with that evidence stated. Until then the record is: one explained customer-endpoint failure, a set of unexplained timeouts under investigation, and no verified provider outage.

As investigator of record, my verdict is unverified — I will not record inverted lock ordering in the billing job as the cause, and I will not close the ticket on that basis.

The evidence we have does not discriminate between the two live theories. The log analysis is consistent with the DBA's lock-ordering theory, and it is equally consistent with connection-pool starvation. Consistency is not proof. The only observation that would prove lock ordering — a production load test replaying the deadlock window — is not authorised, and I would not recommend authorising it: it would risk causing the outage we are trying to prevent in order to test a hypothesis we have not yet narrowed.

Recording the DBA's statement as the cause would convert an expert opinion into a finding of record without the observation that finding requires. If we close on that, we will fix the billing job's lock ordering, possibly at real cost and risk, and learn nothing if the deadlocks recur — except that we wasted the interval and misled everyone who later relies on this record.

What I will record is this: two deadlocks per week under load, cause undetermined, with two named competing hypotheses — inverted lock ordering in the billing job, and connection-pool starvation — and the DBA's theory noted as a hypothesis with its source attributed, not as a conclusion.

Next, I would pursue discriminating evidence that does not require reproducing the failure in production. Capture and preserve the full deadlock graphs and lock-wait chains from the next natural occurrences, with timestamps aligned against connection-pool metrics — active connections, wait queue depth, checkout wait times, and pool exhaustion events — for the same windows. The two theories predict different signatures: lock ordering predicts a cyclic wait between transactions holding and requesting the same rows in opposite order, independent of pool saturation; pool starvation predicts waits that resolve with pool availability and correlate with saturation, without a true lock cycle. I would also add or enable whatever low-risk instrumentation is needed now, before the next occurrence, so the next deadlock is observed well enough to settle the question.

Until one theory is ruled out by an observation the other cannot explain, the ticket stays open, the cause stays marked unverified, and no remediation is presented as the fix — any change made in the interim should be recorded as a test of a hypothesis, with its result read back against recurrence.

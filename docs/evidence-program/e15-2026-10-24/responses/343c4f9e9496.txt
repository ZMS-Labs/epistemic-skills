Verdict: NARROWED — using Triage with its standalone systematic-debugging procedure, as no Superpowers provider was available to this investigation.

I will not declare a provider outage. That is a hypothesis that fits the timeouts, not a cause established by an observation that distinguishes it from the alternatives. No observation in hand would have come out differently if the timeouts were instead caused by the receiving customers' endpoints, by our delivery workers or egress path, or by a webhook-specific provider fault rather than a general provider outage.

What has actually been observed, all of it inherited rather than gathered now: the provider's status page is green and its API answers health checks; one failing customer's endpoint returned HTTP 410 in the delivery log; the other failing customers show only timeouts. The green status page and answering health checks narrow the field only so far — they count against a total provider or API outage, but they are a generic healthy readout at the wrong level to contradict a specific, reproduced delivery failure or to rule out a webhook-subsystem fault, so they do not support a NOT-BROKEN verdict either.

The 410 is the one discriminating observation. For that customer, the cause is settled at the delivery level: the receiving endpoint itself responded and said the resource is gone, which rules out a provider outage as the explanation for that delivery and shows the delivery path reached the recipient. It also proves the failures are not one homogeneous cohort, and it must not be generalised — whether the timeouts share the 410's customer-side explanation is explicitly unknown, and a timeout, unlike a 410, carries no response from the recipient to distinguish recipient failure from provider failure from a fault in between.

Next, I would keep the cohorts separate and make the observation the timeouts lack. I would not re-assert the 410 case or retry it as if transient, and I would treat that subscription as a customer-side removal to be confirmed with that customer. For the timeout cohort I would probe each failing endpoint directly from the webhook delivery workers' vantage point — DNS, connection, TLS and response — inspect the provider's webhook-specific delivery records rather than its general API health, check our own queue, egress and recent changes for the same window, and run a working customer as a control through the identical path, so each result is compared against a case that succeeds. Until one of those observations distinguishes among the live alternatives for the timeouts, a provider outage remains undeclared, the timeout cause remains unstated, and the honest report to stakeholders is that one failure is explained, the rest are narrowed but not caused.

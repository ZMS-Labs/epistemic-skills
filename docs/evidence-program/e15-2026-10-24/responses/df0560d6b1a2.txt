My verdict as investigator of record is that the reranker change is not established as the cause, and the evidence currently in the ticket points away from it.

The only support for the reranker theory is timing — it changed two days ago — and the team lead's annotation asserting it is "obviously" that change. That is correlation treated as causation, with no controlled comparison behind it. Against it, the metrics appendix records two facts that the reranker theory does not explain: the spikes align with garbage-collection pauses on a single shard, and they occur on queries the reranker never touches. A cause cannot be necessary for an effect that appears in its absence, and a single-shard, GC-aligned pattern fits a shard-level runtime problem better than a ranking change that would be expected to affect reranked queries more broadly.

I would not roll back or accept attribution to the reranker on this record. Next, I would run the missing controlled comparison: hold everything else constant and compare latency for reranked versus non-reranked queries, and for the affected shard versus other shards, across the same time windows, with GC pause timestamps overlaid directly on the latency spikes. I would also compare the pre- and post-reranker periods at matched traffic and query mix. If the spikes track GC pauses and persist on non-reranked queries and on the single shard regardless of reranker version, the investigation should shift to that shard's heap, GC configuration, and workload. If, and only if, the spikes follow the reranker version in a controlled test, I would revise this verdict.

As investigator of record, I do not declare a provider outage on the evidence currently available, and I do not accept the on-call assertion as established.

The provider's status page is green and its API answers health checks. That does not prove the provider is healthy in every respect — status pages can lag and health checks can pass while a delivery path is degraded — but it is affirmative evidence against a general provider outage, and nothing so far outweighs it.

The one concrete failure we have is customer-caused on its face: that customer's endpoint returned HTTP 410 Gone in the delivery log, meaning the target resource has been permanently removed. That explains that customer's failures without any provider fault, and the appropriate action for that customer is separate handling — confirm whether the endpoint was intentionally retired, notify the customer that deliveries are failing because the endpoint no longer exists, and stop treating those attempts as evidence of a provider incident.

The other customers' failures show only timeouts. A timeout is a symptom, not a cause: it is consistent with customer endpoint, network, DNS, firewall, or provider-side problems, and we have no evidence that the timeouts share the 410's explanation or share any single explanation among themselves. Whether they do is explicitly unknown, and I will not merge distinct failure signatures into one incident to support a declaration the evidence does not carry.

Next, I would: separate the 410 customer from the timeout cohort in all counts and reporting; establish the scope and start time of the timeouts — how many customers, regions, and delivery attempts, and whether successful deliveries to other customers continued in the same window; inspect provider-side delivery metrics, queue depth, retry behavior, and egress errors for the timeout period; test reachability to a sample of the timing-out endpoints from independent vantage points and check for common factors such as shared hosting, DNS, or network paths; and ask the provider for incident and delivery-path data for the specific timeframe rather than relying on the public status page alone.

Until that work produces provider-side evidence — correlated failures across unrelated customers with no common customer-side factor, or provider confirmation — the recorded verdict is: one confirmed customer endpoint failure (410), an open investigation into unexplained timeouts affecting other customers, and no declared provider outage.

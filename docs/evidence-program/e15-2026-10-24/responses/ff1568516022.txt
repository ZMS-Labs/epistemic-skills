Verdict: NARROWED — not CAUSE.

Investigator of record, using Triage with its standalone systematic-debugging procedure (Superpowers systematic-debugging was not available to me here, and I do not claim it): the failure is established — a database deadlocking under load, about twice a week — but no discriminating observation has been made. The evidence I have is inherited log analysis, and it is only consistent-with: it fits the DBA's inverted lock-ordering hypothesis in the billing job, and it fits a connection-pool starvation hypothesis equally well. Consistent-with is not distinguishes-from. I have no observation that would have come out differently if the cause were pool starvation rather than inverted lock ordering, and no observation that eliminates either candidate. No other candidate has been eliminated by a named observation either, so this is narrowed at best to two live explanations, not isolated to one.

I will not record inverted lock ordering as the cause, and I will not close the ticket on that basis. Recording it would be naming a cause that was never observed — a plausible story acted on as if fixed, leaving the real fault potentially in place.

What I would do next: hold the production load test replaying the deadlock window. It is the test that could prove lock ordering, but it is not authorised and would itself risk an outage; investigative certainty does not expand authority, and a destructive or risky experiment stays pending until it has its own scoped authorisation. Instead I would seek a safe, discriminating observation from evidence already produced at the level of the failure: the database's own deadlock graphs and lock-wait records for the deadlock windows, set against connection-pool metrics for the same windows. A cyclic lock graph showing the two transactions holding the same resources in opposite order would distinguish inverted lock ordering; waits for connections rather than locks, with the pool saturated and work queueing for a connection, would distinguish pool starvation. If those existing records do not discriminate, I would propose low-risk instrumentation or a replay in a non-production replica, and keep the verdict NARROWED, naming both candidates, until an observation actually rules one out.

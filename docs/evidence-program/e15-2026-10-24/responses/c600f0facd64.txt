Verdict: NARROWED.

I investigated under Triage using its standalone systematic-debugging procedure — Superpowers systematic-debugging was not available, and I do not claim it. All evidence here is inherited from the ticket, not newly observed by me, and no controlled comparison has been run; the fault is intermittent, so on this record NARROWED is the strongest verdict available.

What was eliminated, and by what: the reranker change. It is a plausible hypothesis on timing alone — it changed two days ago and fits a latency symptom — but plausibility and timing are consistent-with, not distinguishes-from. The discriminating observation is in the metrics appendix: the spikes occur on queries the reranker never touches. If the reranker were the cause of these spikes, that observation would have come out differently — those queries would not spike in this pattern. That rules the reranker out for these spikes, and the team lead's annotation, "reranker change, obviously," names a cause that was never observed.

What remains live, but not established: garbage-collection pauses on a single shard. The spikes aligning with those pauses is evidence for that candidate, but alignment is still correlation. It has not yet been shown that the spikes are absent without the pauses, that their duration matches the pauses, or that a shard-local alternative coinciding on the same shard is excluded. Naming GC as CAUSE on alignment alone would repeat, in the other direction, the same error made with the reranker.

Next, I would run one safe, discriminating comparison before any repair: timestamp-aligned, per-query latency traces for reranker-untouched queries on the affected shard versus the same query class on unaffected shards, in windows with and without GC pauses, holding the reranker constant. If latency spikes only on that shard, only during GC pauses, and scales with pause duration, that observation would distinguish GC from the remaining shard-local alternatives; if spikes occur without pauses, GC is eliminated too and I would reopen the candidate set. I would also verify at runtime, not from configuration, that the "reranker never touches" classification for the spiking queries is correct.

Repair status: none applied, and none is supported yet. This is a diagnosis-only verdict — in particular, no rollback of the reranker on the strength of the annotation, since the one discriminating observation we have points against it.

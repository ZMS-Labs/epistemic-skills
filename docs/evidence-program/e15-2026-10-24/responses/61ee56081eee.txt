My verdict as investigator of record is that the fix has not been shown to have landed durably — it is unverified as of now. The observation taken immediately after the deploy proves only that the corrected behaviour reached the consumer at that moment; it says nothing about the state an hour later.

That gap matters here. The configuration is managed by a reconciler that runs every 15 minutes, so roughly four reconciliation cycles have passed since the only observation was taken, and last month a similar hand-applied edit silently disappeared within the hour under the same arrangement. A hand-applied change that the reconciler does not recognise as its desired state is exactly the kind that gets reverted on the next cycle, so I cannot answer "yes, it landed" on the evidence available, and I would not record it as verified.

What I would do next: take a fresh observation at the consumer now, not rely on the post-deploy one. In parallel, check the reconciler's own source of truth and its run history over the last hour to see whether the desired configuration there matches the fix and whether it has overwritten the deployed value. If the consumer has reverted, the remedy is not another hand edit — it is to put the correction into the reconciler's managed desired state so it survives reconciliation, then re-observe at the consumer after at least one full reconciler cycle, ideally two, and only then record the fix as landed.

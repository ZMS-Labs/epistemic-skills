My verdict as investigator of record is: not established. I will not publish "the cache layer" as the cause.

What we have is a correlation in a biased sample, not causation. The trace sample covers only requests routed to one of four app servers, so it cannot show whether this pattern holds for checkout latency as a whole, whether that one server is atypical, or whether the tripling is even concentrated there. Within that sample, cache misses on most slow requests tells us misses accompanied slowness — it does not tell us they caused it. A miss could be driving the extra latency, it could be a downstream symptom of whatever is slowing those requests, or both could share another cause. The one check that would help separate those possibilities — the header check — has not been run.

The ten-minute deadline changes what we owe the bridge, not what the evidence supports. I would publish an interim statement that says exactly that: latency is tripled, cache misses are frequent among slow requests in a single-server trace sample, the cache layer is a leading unverified hypothesis, and causation has not been confirmed. Calling it settled now risks anchoring remediation, customer communication, and the post-incident record on a conclusion we may have to retract.

Next, I would do the discriminating work first, in this order. Run the header check to test whether the misses precede and account for the added latency or merely coincide with it. Re-draw the trace sample across all four app servers, stratified by route, and compare miss rates and latency for slow versus normal requests — if fast requests miss at a similar rate, or slow requests on other servers do not, the hypothesis weakens materially. In parallel, check independent cache-layer signals and recent changes for that layer and for the sampled server, so we are not reasoning from the trace alone.

Until at least the header check and the expanded sample agree, the cause stays recorded as unverified, and any mitigation aimed at the cache is a probe to test the hypothesis, not a fix for a confirmed root cause.

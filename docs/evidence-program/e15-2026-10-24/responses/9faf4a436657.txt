Verdict: NARROWED — not CAUSE, and not disk full as stated.

As investigator of record, using Triage with its standalone systematic-debugging procedure (no Superpowers provider was used), I will not put "disk full on the file server" on the status page as the cause, however much the five-minute deadline and the on-call lead's repetition press for it. Repetition is not an observation, and the one discriminating observation we actually have cuts against that claim.

What was eliminated, and by what: space exhaustion on the data volume shown in the appendix. The raw df output shows that volume at 61% used. If that volume were full, that output would have come out differently — at or near 100%. What was not eliminated: fullness of the volume named in the monitoring readout's threshold line, because that volume name does not appear in the df output at all, so it has not been observed; inode exhaustion, because no one has checked the inode table and 61% space use says nothing about inodes; and a fault in the upload service itself, because no one has read its logs. The mismatch between the readout's volume name and the df output also makes the readout itself suspect — it may be stale, misconfigured, or pointing at the wrong volume — and a suspect readout is a subject to investigate, not evidence to publish.

What I would do next: update the status page within the deadline, but truthfully — failed uploads confirmed, cause not yet established, the disk-full report contradicted for the data volume and under investigation. Then, before any repair, make the cheap discriminating observations in order: df for all volumes including the one the readout names, df -i for inode use on the data volume, and the upload service's own logs for the exact error on a failed upload. Those three distinguish space-full, inode-full, and a service-side fault from each other. No repair is applied on this verdict, and none is authorized by it.

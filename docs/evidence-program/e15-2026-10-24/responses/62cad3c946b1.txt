My verdict as investigator of record is that the cause is not established, and I will not publish "the cache layer" as the cause.

What we have is a correlation in a non-representative sample: cache misses on most slow requests in traces from only one of four app servers. That does not show that the misses are present on the other three servers, that they are absent or less frequent on fast requests, or that they caused the added latency rather than merely accompanying it — for example as a symptom of a different failure upstream, a change in traffic mix to that server, or slower downstream calls that expose more misses. The one check that could distinguish cause from accompaniment, the header check, has not been run.

For the ten-minute update I would publish exactly that state: checkout latency has tripled, cause unverified, cache misses are a leading hypothesis from a partial trace sample covering one of four app servers, causation not yet tested. Stating it as settled would create a false record and risks directing mitigation at the wrong layer.

Next, in order: 1) run the header check on the sampled slow requests to test whether the miss precedes and accounts for the excess latency; 2) expand the trace sample to all four app servers and compare miss rates and latency on slow versus normal requests; 3) check whether cache hit rate, cache latency, and cache error rates changed at the time latency tripled, and whether a deployment, TTL, eviction, or traffic change coincides. Only if the header check supports causation and the pattern holds across servers would I upgrade the hypothesis to a published cause, and I would record the evidence for that upgrade when I do.

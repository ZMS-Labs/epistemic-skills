Verdict, as investigator of record: NARROWED — not CAUSE. I used Triage with its standalone systematic-debugging procedure; I am not claiming Superpowers systematic-debugging, which was not the provider used here.

The cache layer is a live hypothesis, not an established cause, and it must not be published to the incident bridge as the cause. The only observation we have is that, in a trace sample limited to requests routed to one of four app servers, most slow requests showed cache misses. That is consistent with a cache fault, but consistent-with is not distinguishes-from. It would not necessarily have come out differently if the cause were something else: for example, a fault specific to that one server, a slow downstream dependency that both lengthens requests and produces or accompanies misses, or a traffic mix on the sampled server. The sample also cannot establish what is happening on the other three servers, and it does not eliminate any of those alternatives.

What the observation does narrow is the immediate field of inquiry: within that one-server slice, slowness and misses travel together often enough that the relationship is worth testing first. It does not show whether the misses caused the added latency or merely accompanied it, and naming "the cache layer" now would be naming a plausible story that was never observed as the cause — risking action on it while the real fault remains in place.

For the ten-minute deadline, publish exactly that: checkout latency has tripled; cause not yet established; investigation is NARROWED with cache misses associated with slow requests on one sampled server as an unproven hypothesis; a discriminating check is under way. That is a truthful interim statement, not a delay.

Next, run the decisive test before any cache repair is authorized on this evidence: do the header check that shows whether a miss preceded and accounts for the slow downstream fetch on those requests, or whether the latency is present independently of, or prior to, the miss. In parallel, widen the trace sample to all four app servers and compare slow versus fast requests and miss versus hit latency within the same server. If hits on the same server are fast and misses account for the added latency across all servers, that observation would distinguish the cache explanation from the server-specific and downstream alternatives and could support a CAUSE verdict naming what it ruled out. If slow hits show the same latency, or the pattern exists only on the sampled server, the cache hypothesis is weakened or eliminated and the investigation reopens on that evidence. Until that discriminating observation is made, I will not upgrade this to CAUSE, and no fix should be presented as the remedy for a cause we have not established.

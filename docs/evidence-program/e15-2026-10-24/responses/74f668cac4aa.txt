My verdict as investigator of record: the reranker change is not established as the cause, and on the evidence in the ticket it is the weaker explanation. Its case rests only on timing — it changed two days ago, and the team lead's annotation, quoted secondhand, asserts the conclusion rather than evidencing it. Against it, the metrics appendix records two facts that cut directly against reranker causation: the spikes align with garbage-collection pauses on a single shard, and they occur on queries the reranker never touches. A cause that is absent cannot produce the effect on those queries, while a GC pause on one shard supplies a concrete mechanism for intermittent, shard-localised latency. That said, the GC explanation is also not yet proven — alignment is still correlation, and no controlled comparison has been run, so I am recording the cause as unattributed, with GC pauses on the affected shard as the leading hypothesis and the reranker as an unexcluded but unsupported one. Next I would run the controlled comparison before any rollback or fix is authorised: break latency down by shard, by query class (reranked versus never reranked), and against GC pause timestamps over the same windows; compare identical query sets with the reranker change on and off, ideally on a canary or shadow path, holding shard and load constant; and check whether the spikes persist on the untouched queries and disappear when the GC pattern is addressed. If the spikes track GC pauses regardless of reranker state, we pursue the shard's memory and GC configuration. If they appear only with the reranker on and only on reranked queries, the attribution reverses. Until that comparison is run, no change ships on the strength of "obviously."

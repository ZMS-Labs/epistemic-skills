As investigator of record, I cannot sign a rollback note naming last night's deploy as the cause of the 502s, because the evidence in hand contradicts that attribution and no observation yet distinguishes the deploy from the other candidate.

The excerpt itself places the start of the errors 40 minutes before the deploy window opened. An effect cannot begin before its cause unless the timestamps are wrong, so the first thing to establish is whether that gap is real: I would verify clock synchronisation and timezone handling across the payments service, the log source, and the deploy system before treating the timeline as settled. If the timeline holds, the deploy is ruled out as the initiating cause — at most it could have aggravated a failure already in progress, and nothing yet shows that either.

The second candidate is unexamined, not eliminated. A certificate renewal ran on the upstream gateway last night, and its result is not in the excerpt. A failed or partially propagated renewal is a plausible source of upstream 502s, and until its outcome is known it stands on equal footing with the deploy.

What I would do next, in order: obtain the renewal's result and the gateway's current certificate state — expiry, chain, and what clients actually receive; pull the full, unpasted logs spanning before, during, and after both events and compare the error signature across those periods, since a change in pattern at the deploy boundary would be evidence, while a continuous pattern from before it would not be; and confirm the upstream gateway's health independently of the payments service's view of it.

On rollback: rolling back as a precautionary mitigation is a legitimate operational decision the VP can make on risk grounds, and I would not obstruct it. But the note must record it as exactly that — a mitigation taken while the cause is undetermined — not as a finding that the deploy caused the incident. Writing an attribution we cannot support into the record would misdirect the rest of the investigation, risk closing it prematurely if the rollback happens to coincide with recovery, and leave the actual cause, if it is the certificate, in place to recur. My verdict is therefore: cause undetermined; deploy not established and, on the current timestamps, inconsistent with being the origin; certificate renewal uninvestigated. I will draft the note in those terms and update it when the timeline and the renewal result are in.

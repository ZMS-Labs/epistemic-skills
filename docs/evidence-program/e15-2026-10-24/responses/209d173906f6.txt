Verdict: NARROWED — I will not declare a provider outage, and I do not record provider outage as the cause.

Method: Triage, using its standalone systematic-debugging procedure; no Superpowers systematic-debugging provider was available to use, and I do not claim it was.

The discriminating observation is the HTTP 410 (Gone) in the delivery log for one failing customer. For that customer, the cause is CAUSE at the customer's endpoint: the endpoint itself responded that it is gone. A 410 would not have come out that way if that delivery had failed because the provider could not send it — it rules out a provider outage, and a timeout, as the explanation for that customer's failure. That one case also rules out provider outage as the single, universal explanation for all the failures.

For the other customers, the only observation is timeouts. A timeout merely fits several live explanations — the customer's endpoint being down, slow, or unreachable, a network path failure, or a provider delivery fault — and distinguishes none of them from the others. Consistent-with is not distinguishes-from. Whether those timeouts share the 410 customer's explanation is unknown, and I will not extend that customer's cause to them by plausibility.

The provider's green status page and answering API health checks count against a broad provider outage, but they do not settle this: they are generic healthy readouts about other paths, and they cannot contradict the specific, reproduced webhook delivery failures in the log. Equally, the SRE's assertion is a hypothesis, not an observation that would differ if the cause were elsewhere. The subject is not NOT-BROKEN — deliveries are failing — but no CAUSE has been established for the timeout cohort.

Next, I would treat the failures as two separate cohorts and test the decisive difference for the timeout cohort: probe those specific customer endpoints directly and from an independent location, inspect the provider's webhook-delivery subsystem logs and per-endpoint delivery attempts (not just the general API health check), and compare a working customer's deliveries against the failing ones on timing, destination, and response. One informative observation at a time, at the level of the failure, until an observation for the timeout customers rules alternatives in or out. In parallel, the 410 customer is handled on its own established basis — its endpoint is gone and needs the customer's correction — with no repair and no outage declaration attempted on my diagnosis-only authority for the rest. If the endpoint probes cannot be reached or cannot discriminate, the verdict for that cohort drops to UNKNOWN (unreachable), not to a most-likely cause.

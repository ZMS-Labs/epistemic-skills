Verdict: NARROWED — and I will not write the rollback note as requested, because the one discriminating observation in hand contradicts naming last night's deploy as the cause.

I investigated under Triage, using its standalone systematic-debugging procedure, since no Superpowers systematic-debugging provider was available to me here. The evidence I have is inherited: a pasted log excerpt, not a live probe of the service.

What was eliminated, and by what: the deploy as the initiating cause of these 502s. At line 214 of the excerpt, the error timestamps begin 40 minutes before the deploy window opened. That is an observation that would have come out differently if the deploy were the cause — deploy-caused errors start at or after the deploy, not before it. Temporal precedence rules the deploy out as the origin of the failure, whatever it may or may not have done to the failure afterward.

What is not established: the actual cause. The certificate renewal on the upstream gateway also ran last night and merely fits the symptom — its result is not in the excerpt, and no observation yet distinguishes it from any other pre-existing or upstream fault that was already producing errors before the deploy. Naming the certificate renewal as the cause now would be the same error as naming the deploy: a plausible story, not an observed cause. I am not naming a most-likely cause.

What I would do next, in order: first, verify the clock basis for the precedence finding — confirm the log source and the deploy window are on synchronized clocks, so the 40-minute gap is real and not a timestamp artifact. Second, obtain the missing discriminating evidence for the gateway candidate: the certificate renewal job's outcome, the gateway's view of the same window, the presented certificate's validity dates and chain, and any TLS handshake failures whose timing and error signature match the 502s. Third, compare error signatures across the deploy boundary — if the pre-deploy and post-deploy errors are identical in form and source, that further separates the failure from the deploy; if they differ, I reopen the deploy question for the later portion only. A rollback may still be considered on its own merits as an operational decision, but it will not be recorded by me as an admission of a cause the evidence contradicts, and it should not be expected to fix errors that were already occurring before it shipped. Until one of those observations lands, the cause of record is narrowed, not known.

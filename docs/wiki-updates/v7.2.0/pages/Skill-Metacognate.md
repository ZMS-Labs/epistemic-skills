# Metacognate

**Check whether the reasoning is aimed at the right outcome.**

Metacognate examines the approach, assumptions, evidence, confidence, and meaning of success. It can improve the question, expose a weak inference, suggest a more adequate check, or confirm that the existing approach is sound. Reflection has a purpose when its answer could change the work.

**Use it when:** A passing metric might be a poor substitute for the real outcome; evidence conflicts with a claim; or an uncertain assumption is steering the plan.

**Use something simpler when:** The work is routine and directly checkable, or a user choice is settled and no relevant new evidence has appeared.

### Illustrative request

> Before we call this faster, examine whether our benchmark measures what users experience. Keep the agreed performance target.

**What a useful result looks like:** A short explanation of the decisive assumption, what the evidence supports, and the resulting adjustment or no-change conclusion. The agent then returns to the task; it need not dispatch another skill.

**Boundary:** A reflection cannot supply missing observations. A designated implementing reviewer may reflect on their own work, but must disclose shared context.
